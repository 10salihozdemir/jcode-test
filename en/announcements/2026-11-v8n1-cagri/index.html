For authors

Call text

JCoDe has previously considered artificial intelligence in two instances: first when it was seen as an emerging area of study in architecture (in 2020) and then as a challenge to human-centered design (in 2024). Ever since, AI has changed from being a tool that designers employ to becoming a participant that generates, evaluates, and reframes design itself. In response, the sixteenth issue of JCoDe adopts a deliberately open approach by using the phrase Design [AI] Design. The bracket is the issue in question. In editorial practice, square brackets are used to indicate something that has been inserted into someone else's text. In phenomenology, bracketing involves suspending a phenomenon so that what remains can be examined. In the field of computation, brackets are used for indexing, nesting, and for making calls. And when positioned between two meanings of design, AI can be interpreted in more than one way too.

  • as a mediator that translates design into design;
  • as an insertion that totally alters authorship;
  • as an operator that makes design recursive;
  • as a variable, its suspension shows what design has always been.

Every time we read new questions arise. Who or what is carrying out the design when design is influenced by systems that are in turn designed? How are intention, judgment and responsibility shared among human and machine actors? What becomes of design knowledge, representation and teaching when the cycle between designing and being designed comes to an end? In this context, the sixteenth issue of JCoDe includes discussions concerning theoretical, methodological, empirical and practice-oriented contributions in which AI is not seen simply as a tool to be used, but rather as a condition that alters design thinking, the processes involved, and the results. Possible topics include, but are not limited to:

  • Meta-design: designing the models, datasets and systems that design
  • Language, prompting and dialogue between designers and AI
  • Representation, abstraction and reframing in generative workflows
  • AI in design education and studio pedagogy
  • Evaluation, critique and judgment of AI-generated design
  • Ethical, cultural and ecological implications of AI-mediated design
  • Design automation and computational creativity
  • Embodied, spatial and immersive interaction with AI systems

The sixteenth issue of ITU Faculty of Architecture E-Journal JCoDe: Journal of Computational Design is planned to be published in March 2027. The journal is peer-reviewed and published twice a year (September-March). Articles can be in Turkish or English. The author guidelines can be accessed from the following links.
